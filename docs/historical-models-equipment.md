# Historical equipment models

Status: implementation and visual-defect repair for the authorized `codex/v072-historical-models` server worktree, 2026-09-12. The first real WebGL gallery exposed inward-wound shared chamfered shells and unsupported elevated auxiliary mounts despite passing structural tests. This document records the bounded repair; final gallery acceptance, full-suite totals and release packaging are recorded by the release owner separately.

## Scope and model identity

The new renderer covers five exterior categories and the three existing interior inspection categories. It uses original procedural geometry; no commercial meshes, textures, or model downloads are incorporated.

- CL/BB main batteries select their housing profile by ship class: Cleveland Mk 16; Edinburgh Mk XXIII; Nürnberg SK C/25; Agano 41st Year; Dido QF 5.25; North Carolina Mk 6; King George V Mk VII; Bismarck SK C/34; Yamato Type 94; Richelieu Mle 1935. Their four equipment rarity levels remain performance upgrades, not four different historical guns or paint colours.
- DDs retain freely fitted equipment identities, not fictitious original class armament: Mk IX/CP XVIII single, Japanese Type C twin, US Mk 38 twin, and British RP10 twin. The actual source `mainGunId` and `visual.barrelCount` govern geometry. A DD fitted with a foreign component remains a player build.
- Turrets use separate roller paths, barbettes, clipped gunhouses or open-backed shields, sighting hoods, roof profiles and rangefinder massing. Main barrels are stepped circular meshes with recessed bores, individually animated by the existing recoil system.
- The Type C twin uses a long sloping forward face, Mk 38 a boxier aft body and sloping forward roof, and RP10 a compact multi-section faceted shell. They no longer use almost identical rounded caps. These are artist-selected family silhouettes, not traced measured reconstructions; existing barrel and housing dimensions are retained.
- Torpedo launchers have model-specific open or shielded mounts, saddles, reinforcing bands, breech doors and training equipment. Tube counts remain the game's existing 2/3/5/4 for Mk IX/G7a/Mk 15/Type 93. The British twin and German triple are explicitly game abstractions, not claims about the standard J-class or Type 1936 installations.
- AA silhouettes are single Oerlikon with drum and shield, octuple pom-pom with stacked barrels and feed boxes, twin Bofors with loading chutes, and quad Bofors with a separate director representation. The quad reference manual below describes Mk 4, while the catalogue names Mk 2: shared-family characteristics are used, not an assertion that these mounts are interchangeable in every detail.
- Secondary guns are open-shield SK C/33 twin, enclosed QF 5.25 twin, faceted SK C/28 twin and Mk 16 triple. Depth weapons are stern release rails, a Japanese thrower representation, K-gun with ready charges, and a 24-spigot Hedgehog representation. No additional simulated shots are created by decorative parts.
- Interior inspection shows magazine racks/hoists, turbine casings/reduction gears/pipes, and hydraulic rams/pumps/reservoirs. These are compact structural diagrams, not measured room layouts. All four catalogue engines are steam systems; no existing engine ID is converted into a diesel plant. Exact Parsons/Kampon/Wagner layouts are not established by the US DD machinery source.

## Coordinate and ownership contract

`createLoadoutEquipmentVisual` keeps its public signature and result arrays. The adapter consumes the resolved plan's equipment IDs, slot indices, positions and headings. Each exterior mount now has an **unrotated mount frame** positioned at the normalized hardpoint, scaled by the reciprocal of the ship's render scale. The returned turret/launcher is its rotating child, at local position zero. This cancels the hull's nonuniform scale before weapon rotation: all exterior visual/profile dimensions are interpreted as metres. It does not alter saved builds, weapon damage/reload/speed, projectile counts or the LAN protocol. The explicitly authorized simulation change brings muzzle origins and pitch into the same physical frame.

For each main gun the cradle `(0, 1.45, 0.75)`, barrel rotation `(π/2, 0, 0)`, barrel centre `z = 0.47L + 0.55`, length `L`, and offset `(i − (n−1)/2) × spacing` are retained, now in metres. `mainBatteryBarrelRestZ` and `mainBatteryMuzzleOffset` are the shared geometric contract. The forward tip distance at zero pitch/recoil is `0.97L + 1.3`. `gunMountElevation(ship, index)` derives pitch from a nonrecursive cradle base and `ballisticVelocity`, clamped to the existing 0–0.34 radians; both GameView's individual cradles and simulation muzzle offsets use that pitch. Damage, reload, muzzle velocity and salvo size are not changed.

The actual mesh test reads the muzzle-ring vertex height (not metadata or the simulation offset helper), transforms it through the full scene hierarchy, and compares it to `gunMuzzleOrigins` within 0.001 m. It covers 15 classes, mixed equipment, empty slots, three ship headings and three turret yaw offsets, with nonzero elevation. Separate tests retain the primitive barrel/recoil local contract. The comparison uses the authoritative ship pose and the unrecoiled firing position; existing cosmetic sea bob, roll and sinking/settling animations are deliberately not added to physics.

Static exterior parts are assembled in metres and merged **before** attaching to the supplied metre frame. The ship-root scale is neither baked into the vertices nor re-applied to the weapon. Interior models continue to use the existing hull-normalized compartment bounds. Shared palette materials are reused; rarity does not select a new material. Main mounts have one static merged mesh plus one mesh per animated barrel: 2–4 draw calls for single/twin/triple, and a documented 5-call exception for quad mounts. Other mounts and internal models have two merged meshes. The focused tests confirm these structural counts; they are not measured frame-time results.

The shared chamfered-box caps and walls use outward Babylon left-handed winding, with backface culling retained. Regression tests check actual vertex normals, nondegenerate triangles, signed volume, and paired oppositely directed edges after welding positional seams. Separate tests apply the same contracts to the three new asymmetric DD shells.

Elevated AA and secondary mounts receive circular supported platforms, tapered pedestals and deck feet. Footing height interpolates the historical hull's station sheer/forecastle edge and extends 0.12 m below that edge, intersecting the crowned deck. Supports are merged into the fitting's existing structure-material mesh: no extra draw call per mount, no duplicate main barbette, and no change to simulation hardpoints, reciprocal mount frames or muzzle transforms. A regression test intersects rays with actual rendered deck geometry across all fifteen ship classes, compares support vertex heights, and checks unchanged mount placement and two-mesh auxiliary batches.

`createHistoricalInternalEquipment(scene, parent, name, module, palette)` returns an independently disposable `TransformNode`, positioned at `module.position`, with `inspectionOnly: true` metadata. Battle construction never calls this helper. Dock inspection assigns its existing through-hull marker material to the model meshes, retaining `Constants.ALWAYS` and render group 3; the one-box substitute is removed. Closing or replacing inspection disposes only that overlay.

Root metadata includes `historicalProfile` and the source equipment identity. Merged mesh metadata includes `historicalParts`, a list of the geometry pieces actually built. Main barrels expose their original local muzzle point in metadata for gallery inspection. Metadata is diagnostic, not a substitute for vertex/bounds checks.

### Corrected catalogue attribution

The common gun remains Mk IX / CP Mk XVIII, a single mount. The erroneous J/K/N attribution has been corrected to the H/I/O/Q/R family in the base catalogue and seven localized origin strings by the catalogue owner. The Royal Navy source above confirms Mk IX** / XVIII. The secondary reference [NavWeaps Mk IX/Mk XII table](https://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WNBR_47-45_mk9.php) distinguishes XVIII single mounts from XIX twin Mk XII mounts for J/K/N. Equipment identity and barrel count are unchanged.

## Acceptance blockers and follow-up

1. Repair validation passed on the authorized Ubuntu worktree: `npx tsc --noEmit` and `npx vitest run tests/chamferedBoxGeometry.test.ts tests/historicalEquipment.test.ts tests/historicalMuzzleAlignment.test.ts tests/mainBattery.test.ts tests/dockLoadoutRenderer.test.ts tests/loadoutVisualPlan.test.ts --maxWorkers=1`, **84/84 tests across six files**, 6.95 s test-run duration. This includes five new primitive/asymmetric-shell tests and the new AA/secondary footing test across all fifteen classes. The footing ray is restricted to the weather-deck area because the shared deck-material batch also contains upper bridge roofs. Full-suite and packaging runs remain release-owner work.
2. The previous capital-ship dimension multiplication is resolved by the reciprocal mount frame: North Carolina's declared 9.1 m gunhouse is no longer expanded to 27.3 m solely by hull scale, and the 13.5 m source barrel remains 13.5 m after attachment/rotation. This is a consistent unit interpretation, not a claim that every source number was measured from a drawing. Housing depth/height/rangefinder values remain artist-selected metre-scale approximations requiring visual review.
3. The previous `0.88L × hull scale` simulation formula is replaced by the shared metre offset and pitch. The actual-mesh tests cover each barrel's lateral spacing and fixed cradle/rest offsets. Cosmetic hull motions and visible post-shot recoil remain presentation-only and outside the authoritative firing-origin comparison.
4. A real geometry gallery was rendered and reviewed, exposing the shell-winding and elevated-support defects addressed here. A fresh affected-equipment/equipped-capital-ship pass must confirm this repair. Quad turrets retain the intentional five-mesh animation exception; passing a browser-error or screenshot-generation check alone is not visual acceptance.
5. German and several British/French capital-turret forms currently have illustrative profile distinctions, not a complete traced set of museum plans. Additional primary image verification is needed before claiming an exact historical reconstruction. The interior national variants are expressly schematic and should be labelled accordingly in any gallery.
